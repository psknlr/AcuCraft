---
id: pattern-differentiation
title: Pattern differentiation that rests on its findings
when: Before you name a TCM pattern in assess.
---
A pattern is a conclusion. Here it is recorded with the findings it rests on, and it is read from what those findings show — never from its name, and never from the diagnosis.

1. **Know what the candidates are recognised by.** `lookup pattern` gives, for each pattern, its key signs, its supporting signs and the signs that speak against it, each with the examination that finds it (inquiry, inspection, tongue, pulse, palpation). It also gives the rule for how much is enough.
2. **Gather what bears on them.** The four examinations, as far as they apply: ask about the character of the complaint and what changes it (cold, damp, heat, rest, movement, pressure, time of day), about sleep, appetite, stools, thirst, mood and energy; inspect, and look at the tongue (body and coat); palpate the affected part, and feel the pulse. A sign you did not look for is unknown — it is not absent, and it cannot be cited.
3. **Choose on the findings.** Which pattern do the findings you have actually show? If two fit, look for the finding that tells them apart. If what you found speaks against your first idea, change the idea.
4. **Record it with its evidence.** `assess` with `pattern` and `patternEvidence`: the ids of the answers, examination findings and reports the pattern rests on (they are in `view` record). Name the findings that carry it; naming everything you have does not make it stronger, and leaving out a finding that speaks against it does not remove it.
5. **Revise.** A later finding that changes the picture calls for a new `assess`. The latest one is the assessment on record.

You are not told whether a differentiation holds. If there is not enough to carry any pattern, record the diagnosis without one rather than a pattern that rests on nothing.
